Prestige and scarcity are not the same thing. In Accra as elsewhere, a location can be famous, expensive, and still lose ground to a quieter address one district over. This matters especially for diaspora capital, where the exit is measured in years and the mistake is measured in dollars.

## The three variables

We underwrite on three primary axes.

**1. Constrained supply.** Not just current supply, but the pipeline of consented sites within a fifteen-minute drive. In prime Accra — Airport Residential, Cantonments, Labone, Roman Ridge — the finite-plot arithmetic is the whole story.

**2. Migrating demand.** Are the households and businesses moving *into* the catchment younger, wealthier, or more mobile than the ones leaving? Diaspora principals coming home skew the answer at the top of the market; institutional tenants shape the middle.

**3. Infrastructure delta.** Public investment — power, water, transport, fibre — arrives ten years before it prices in. Underwriting only what is already visible on the ground is a slow way to lose. Underwriting the *committed pipeline* is how the compounding starts.

Great locations compound. Prestigious ones frequently do not. The best advice a real-estate advisor can give a diaspora principal is which of the two they are actually buying.
