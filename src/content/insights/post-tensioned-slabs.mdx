Post-tensioned (PT) concrete slabs are frequently proposed as a first-line answer to any span problem. In practice they earn their keep in a narrower set of conditions than the marketing suggests.

## When PT wins

- Column grids above ~8.5 m in both directions
- Deflection-sensitive fit-outs (dance floors, MRI suites, glazed floor plates)
- Programme constraints where slab-cycle time is on the critical path

## When it doesn't

- Small footprints where the tendon layout and stressing sequence consume the schedule saving
- Frames with complex penetrations or late-stage services coordination
- Sites where the specialist stressing sub-contractor market is thin

The honest answer, in a room where the client is paying for the truth rather than a preferred outcome, is that a well-detailed conventional RC frame beats a poorly executed PT slab every time. The engineering job is to know which case is in front of you.
